# Minimal Invariant Relational Units Across Oscillation and Robust Adaptation
## Cross-domain paper draft seed

### Abstract
Systems theory lacks a generally adjudicable operational unit whose identity resides in relations rather than component inventory. We test a preregistered intervention tribunal—substitution, rewiring, ablation, transport and jurisdictional minimality—on two unrelated functions. In a synthetic gene oscillator, the tribunal identifies closed three-node cyclic repression as the minimal invariant carrier of sustained phase-ordered oscillation. In robust perfect adaptation, the same protocol identifies a constant-signal internal-model closure: sensed output error is integrated into persistent memory and returned through corrective feedback. The second carrier survives substitutions across plant order, nonlinear chemotaxis dynamics and antithetic biomolecular control; all proper relation subsets and frozen rewires fail. Published experiments transport the relation across bacterial chemotaxis, synthetic bacterial regulation, cell-free systems, mammalian gene circuits and engineered controllers. The method therefore identifies known design invariants without privileging a single material substrate or dynamic function. We call the resulting operational equivalence class a minimal invariant relational unit (UMI). The work does not claim a new ontology, discovery of integral control, or empirical multifractality.

### Main result

\[
\mathcal U_{\rm osc}=\text{closed three-repression cycle},
\]

\[
\mathcal U_{\rm RPA}=\text{constant-signal internal-model closure}.
\]

The former preserves an oscillatory phase signature; the latter preserves robust perfect adaptation. Their common status is relational minimality and transportability, not common components or common equations.
