# HGMF‑UMI‑22 — Scale Identifiability Theorem

## Independent intervention scale

A level counts as an independent intervention scale only if:

1. it is produced by a physical intervention rather than analytical rebinning;
2. a refinement or nesting relation is declared;
3. sufficient replication or branching exists;
4. depth is not determined by another confounded control;
5. a homologous semantic map exists between carriers;
6. the observation passes the resolution gate.

## Linear-chain no-go

For a hierarchy with one node at each level, normalized mass occupies one cylinder. Thus

\[
Z(q,k)=1
\]

for all moments and depths, and

\[
\tau(q)=0.
\]

Increasing cycle count alone cannot establish a nontrivial multifractal hierarchy.

## Confounded-depth no-go

If intervention control \(u_k\) is a one-to-one function of historical depth \(k\), and no crossed repetitions exist, control and history effects are not separately identifiable.

## Segment-count no-go

Sibling loops, repeated returns and multiple segments increase event count without necessarily increasing tree depth.

## Cross-carrier bottleneck

The transportable scale range is bounded by the carrier with the smallest independent hierarchy and by the existence of a homologous ancestry map. Failure in either carrier requires abstention from a cross-carrier spectrum claim.
