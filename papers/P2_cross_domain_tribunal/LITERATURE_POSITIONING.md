# P2 Literature Positioning and Novelty Boundary

## 1. Comparator matrix

| Framework | Established contribution | Overlap with UMI | Residual delta of P2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Network motifs | Statistically overrepresented subgraphs and candidate building blocks across network classes | Relational architecture may nominate a carrier | No overrepresentation requirement; topology alone is insufficient; requires semantic freeze, kinetic/physical jurisdiction, negative interventions, transport and minimality |
| Functional / dynamical motifs | Recurring dynamic computations, pathways or modules | Function-bearing relational patterns | P2 adds one cross-domain tribunal and explicit decoy, abstention and substrate-transport tests |
| Design principles | Minimal models or architectures sufficient for a declared function | Function-relative mechanism classes | P2 adjudicates whether a design principle is also inclusion-minimal, invariant and transportable under a frozen evidence grammar |
| Minimal realization | Smallest state-space realization of external behavior | Minimality and equivalence | P2 minimality is relative to a semantic signature and intervention family, not complete input–output behavior |
| Bisimulation | Behavioral equivalence finer than simple output equality | Equivalence across representations | P2 does not require full bisimulation; it tests a specified task and intervention class and may admit weaker functional transport |
| Internal model principle | Necessary mechanism for robust regulation of a signal class | Strong UMI evidence for RPA | Domain theorem, not a general cross-domain unit tribunal |
| RPA basis modules | Topological basis for robust perfect adaptation | Carrier classes and refinement | Restricted to adaptation; P2 asks whether identical adjudication logic works across unrelated functions |
| Organisational closure | Mutual dependence among constraints that sustain biological organisation | Closure and relation-first unity | Stronger biological/autonomy commitments; P2 carriers need not be self-producing, autonomous or living |
| Causal/interventionist mechanism | Necessity and causal contribution under manipulation | Ablation and rewiring | P2 adds substitution invariance, cross-substrate transport, inclusion-minimality, strict semantic freeze and abstention |
| RAF theory | Formal autocatalytic and food-generated closure | One exact chemical UMI class | Domain-specific closure formalism rather than a general carrier criterion |
| Elementary flux modes / minimal cut sets | Minimal metabolic pathways or intervention sets under stoichiometric constraints | Minimality and failure interventions | Specialized to metabolic network constraints; semantic transport across substrates is not the core target |

## 2. Load-bearing antecedents

### Structural motifs

Milo et al. introduced network motifs as interconnection patterns statistically enriched relative to randomized networks and suggested that such motifs may act as universal building blocks. This is the most important structural antecedent. P2 must not imply that relational building blocks are new as a broad idea.

**Reference:** Milo R, Shen-Orr S, Itzkovitz S, Kashtan N, Chklovskii D, Alon U. “Network motifs: simple building blocks of complex networks.” *Science* 298 (2002): 824–827.

### Structure does not determine function

Ingram, Stumpf and Stark showed that a single biological motif can display widely different or opposite dynamics depending on parameters and internal realization. This result directly supports the P2 requirement that a carrier include a kinetic or physical jurisdiction rather than a bare graph.

**Reference:** Ingram PJ, Stumpf MPH, Stark J. “Network motifs: structure does not determine function.” *BMC Genomics* 7, 108 (2006).

### Dynamic and compositional motifs

Prill, Iglesias and Levchenko linked motif abundance to robust dynamical properties, while recent category-theoretic work by Aduddell et al. relates signed regulatory graphs, reaction networks and parameterized dynamics compositionally. These narrow the novelty boundary: P2 cannot claim to be the first bridge from topology to dynamics or the first compositional account of motifs and mechanisms.

**References:**

- Prill RJ, Iglesias PA, Levchenko A. “Dynamic Properties of Network Motifs Contribute to Biological Network Organization.” *PLoS Biology* 3 (2005).
- Aduddell R, Fairbanks JP, Kumar A, Ocal PS, Patterson E, Shapiro B. “A compositional account of motifs, mechanisms, and dynamics in biochemical regulatory networks.” *Compositionality* 6, 2 (2024 publication record; work circulated in 2023).

### Bisimulation and minimal realization

Van der Schaft formalized bisimulation for dynamical systems as a unifying relation between state-space equivalence and reduction. Realization theory establishes minimal representations of external behavior. P2 must therefore describe UMI equivalence as task- and intervention-relative, not as a replacement for these stronger equivalence theories.

**References:**

- van der Schaft AJ. “Equivalence of dynamical systems by bisimulation.” *IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control* 49 (2004): 2160–2172.
- Silverman LM. “Realization of linear dynamical systems.” *IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control* 16 (1971): 554–567.

### Robust perfect adaptation

Araujo and Liotta established two topological module classes forming a basis for robust perfect adaptation in networks of arbitrary size. Bhattacharya, Raman and Tangirala derived control-theoretic constraints and minimum order for adaptation-capable biological networks. These are stronger domain-specific results than P2’s RPA worked case. P2 uses them as evidence; it does not supersede them.

**References:**

- Araujo RP, Liotta LA. “The topological requirements for robust perfect adaptation in networks of any size.” *Nature Communications* 9 (2018).
- Bhattacharya P, Raman K, Tangirala AK. “Discovering adaptation-capable biological network structures using control-theoretic approaches.” *PLoS Computational Biology* 18 (2022).

### Closure of constraints

Montévil and Mossio formalized biological organisation as closure among mutually dependent constraints. The overlap with UMI is real but partial. Their framework addresses biological organisation and self-maintenance; P2 uses a weaker, task-relative closure applicable to nonliving and informational mechanisms.

**Reference:** Montévil M, Mossio M. “Biological organisation as closure of constraints.” *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 372 (2015): 179–191.

## 3. Defensible methodological delta

The strongest novelty claim is not any individual element. Substitution, intervention, transport, minimality and equivalence all have established histories. The defensible delta is their **conjunctive operationalization** as one frozen tribunal with the following properties:

1. the semantic signature is preregistered and is part of the unit definition;
2. candidates are tested by both preservation and destruction interventions;
3. carrier identity is separated from component identity by substitution and cross-substrate transport;
4. cardinality-preserving rewiring distinguishes relations from inventory;
5. minimality is by inclusion and relative to jurisdiction;
6. multiple carriers and insufficient evidence produce class-valued outcomes or abstention;
7. one decision grammar is applied across dynamical, informational, control, topological and chemical mechanisms;
8. designed and naturalistic decoys explicitly test mechanism–phenotype confusion;
9. refinement relations distinguish parent and stronger carrier classes.

## 4. Claims that would be defeated by prior literature

P2 must not claim:

- first identification of recurring relational building blocks;
- first demonstration that topology and function differ;
- first concept of dynamical or functional modules;
- first theory of minimal system realization or behavioral equivalence;
- first formalization of internal models, RPA modules or biological closure;
- first use of perturbation or ablation to identify mechanism;
- first recognition of substrate-independent computation or multiple realization.

## 5. Claims that remain supportable

Subject to final priority audit, P2 may claim:

- a unified, explicitly frozen intervention tribunal joining these previously separated criteria;
- a cross-domain carrier atlas using one decision logic;
- benchmark evidence that semantic under-specification causes false positives;
- an abstention rule for incomplete or causally nonunique cases;
- a refinement-order view of relational units;
- reproducible claim ledgers connecting definitions, interventions, transport evidence and withheld claims.
